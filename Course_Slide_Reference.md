# Course Slide Reference (Week 2) for the Milestone 3 Report

Frameworks from the lecturer's Week 2 slides that the Milestone 3 (Customer and Market Validation) report uses. Slide numbers are from "Customer Discovery and Market Research (Week 2 - Session 1)" unless stated otherwise.

## Problem statement formula (slide 15)

"[Customer] experiences [Problem] when [Situation], causing [Consequence]. They currently use [Alternatives]."

## Beachhead segment (slide 16)

"All students" is not a target segment. Start with the specific segment that feels the strongest pain, and explain why that pain is strong enough to drive adoption.

## Five lenses for choosing a segment (slide 17)

1. Situation
2. Behaviour
3. Pain intensity
4. Accessibility
5. Founder fit

Founder fit multiplies demand but does not replace it.

## Market research (slides 19–21)

- Market research turns assumptions into evidence (slide 19).
- Combine primary research (interviews, surveys, observation) with secondary research (reports, studies, competitor benchmarking) (slide 20).
- Choose the method for the research question: behaviour ("what") or motivation ("why") (slide 21).

## Evidence Ladder, strongest first (slide 22)

1. Commitment
2. Existing effort
3. Recent problem
4. Stated interest
5. Opinion

What people do is more valuable than what they say.

## Interviews (slides 23–25)

- Ask about past behaviour, not hypothetical situations ("Tell me about a time…" instead of "What would you do if…") (slide 24).
- Interview Journey (slide 25): Warm-up → Context → Recent experience → Problem → Alternative → Effort → Closing. Turn leading questions ("Don't you think…?") into neutral ones ("How would you describe…?").

Used in `03_Market_Validation/KoraFlow_Interview_Guide.pdf`, where each question is labelled with its stage.

## Surveys (slides 26–28)

- A discovery survey focuses on the problem rather than pitching a solution (slide 26).
- Survey rules (slide 27):
  - Start with screening questions.
  - One idea per question.
  - Neutral wording.
  - Mutually exclusive answer choices.
  - Ask specific behavioural questions.
  - Pilot the survey with a small group first.
- Relevance beats volume (slide 28).

## Competitors (slides 29–32)

- Competition is defined by the customer's problem. Types: direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo / substitutes, meaning the choice to "do nothing" (slides 29–30).
- A missing feature is not automatically a market gap. A real gap needs active complaints, a documented search for solutions and a demonstrated willingness to pay (slide 31).
- Compare options on price, time, quality and convenience (slide 32).

Used in `03_Market_Validation/KoraFlow_Competitor_Analysis.pdf`.

## Decision after validation (slides 33–34)

- **Persevere:** repeated high-intensity problem, weak alternatives, meaningful action.
- **Refine:** the problem exists, but the timing, format, price or segment differs from the assumption.
- **Pivot:** another segment or problem shows stronger evidence than the original.
- **Pause:** the problem is rare, low-consequence or already solved.

Used as the pre-agreed decision rule in the interview guide and the report.

## Use of AI (Week 2 Session 2, slide 6)

"AI supports thinking; it does not provide real customer evidence." All evidence in the report must come from real survey responses and interviews.
